# Limitations

*Plain-language note: every study has limits. These are this project's, stated plainly so that no one, including you, claims more than the data support. The paper's limitations section is written from this list. *

1. **One trial, one institution.** All 1,019 students attended one large Hispanic-Serving university over three semesters. Results may differ elsewhere. This project does not yet measure national effects; that is version 2 (see NEXT_STEPS.md).
2. **Instructors are tied to teaching style.** 7 instructors taught only active sections and 12 taught only lecture sections. Some of the measured difference could reflect who taught rather than how. Clustering by section widens the uncertainty to reflect this but cannot remove it.
3. **Unequal missing test scores.** 21.3% of lecture students and 9.8% of active-learning students who stayed have no end-of-term score. Lee bounds show the gain is at least 11.7 points under the least favorable assumption, but those bounds assume active learning did not cause any student to skip the test who otherwise would have taken it.
4. **Grades are not blind.** Course grades were assigned by instructors who knew which style they taught. The common learning measure is more standardized than grades.
5. **Students who switched sections have no outcomes.** 208 allocated students did not remain in their assigned section. Results describe students who stayed. Stay rates were similar in both groups (80.8% and 78.3%).
6. **Subgroup results are exploratory.** Some groups are small (for example, 104 Black or African American students), and many comparisons were made. No group showed a reliably different effect.
7. **Small count discrepancy.** The data README reports 373 active-learning students with a learning score; the file contains 376. All analyses use the file as released.
8. **Reuse terms.** The Zenodo record is CC0, but the data README says the data "may be used for research purposes only." This project is research and honors that request.
